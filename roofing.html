WHY ROOFS ACTUALLY LEAK

The stain is inside. The cause is usually somewhere else.

Most leaks don't start where the water shows up. Failed flashing around a chimney or vent, worn underlayment, or a small gap at a valley can carry water well past the visible entry point before it ever reaches a ceiling.

Does a water stain mean I need a new roof?Not necessarily — many stains trace back to a localized, repairable failure point.
Do missing shingles always mean replacement?Isolated shingle loss is often a repair if the surrounding roof and decking are sound.
Will a repair hold long-term?It depends on the roof's overall age and condition — findings from an inspection make that clear.
REPAIR OR REPLACE?

We tell you which one your roof actually needs.

Replacement is only the right recommendation when repair genuinely won't hold. Both are documented in your written findings.

REPAIR MAY BE WORTH DOING

The roof is still fundamentally sound.

  • Damage is localized — a section, not the whole roof.
  • Decking and structure underneath are in good condition.
  • Flashing or ventilation issues can be corrected directly.
  • The remaining shingle life supports a targeted fix.
REPLACEMENT MAY BE THE RIGHT CALL

The problem is broader than one spot.

  • Widespread age-related wear across the whole roof.
  • Multiple past repairs with recurring leaks.
  • Decking damage found during inspection.
  • Storm damage extensive enough that repair won't hold.

FREQUENTLY ASKED

Roof repair & replacement questions

Straight answers to what homeowners ask us most.

How do I know if I need a repair or a full replacement?

It comes down to how widespread the damage is and the condition of the decking underneath. Localized damage on an otherwise sound roof is usually a repair. Widespread wear, repeated leaks in different spots, or damaged decking point toward replacement. Our inspection findings spell out which situation applies to your roof.

What are the warning signs my roof needs attention?

Water stains on ceilings or in the attic, missing or curling shingles, granules collecting in gutters, daylight visible through the roof deck, and a roof approaching or past its expected lifespan are all reasons to schedule an inspection.

How fast can someone come out for a leak?

Active leaks are treated as urgent — call the number above rather than booking a standard inspection slot. For non-urgent concerns, inspections are typically scheduled within a few business days.

What actually happens during an inspection?

We examine the roof surface, flashing, vents and, where accessible, the attic and decking, documenting condition with photos. You receive written findings explaining what we found and what — if anything — needs to happen next.

How long does a replacement take?

Most residential replacements are completed in one to a few days depending on roof size, pitch, material and weather. We'll give you a specific timeline as part of your written findings, not a generic estimate.

Could my damage be storm-related and covered by insurance?

Wind and hail damage isn't always visible from the ground. If a storm has recently passed through your area, a documented inspection gives you a record you can bring to your insurer — we don't handle claims ourselves, but our findings can support one.

How is my property protected during the work?

Crews use tarps and ground protection for landscaping, gutters and driveways, and a magnetic sweep is run for debris and nails after the job. Details specific to your property are covered before work begins.

What warranty comes with the work?

Work is backed by a workmanship warranty, separate from any manufacturer material warranty on the shingles or components installed. Specific terms are included in your written findings and estimate.
