WHY CONCRETE SINKS

The visible problem is above. The cause may be below.

Settlement can involve changes in soil or base support, water movement, erosion, compaction, moisture and voids beneath the slab. The useful question is not simply “How do we lift it?” but “Why did it move?”

Is a hollow sound important?It can suggest lost contact or a void, but it is not a complete diagnosis by itself.
Will leveling fix drainage?It can improve a low point caused by settlement, but separate drainage problems may also need correction.
Will it sink again?Future movement depends partly on the underlying cause and the stability of support beneath the slab.
PRESERVE OR REPLACE?

Can the slab be saved?

Leveling is most useful when settlement is the main problem and the concrete is still worth keeping.

LEVELING MAY BE WORTH EVALUATING

The slab is substantially intact.

  • Settlement is the primary defect.
  • The surface is still useful if elevation is restored.
  • A lift can improve a trip edge, transition or drainage.
  • The underlying support problem can be reasonably addressed.
ANOTHER SOLUTION MAY BE BETTER

The problem is not just elevation.

  • Severe deterioration or fragmentation.
  • Surface failure across much of the slab.
  • Upward displacement such as root pressure.
  • A condition that lifting cannot correct safely.
HOW LEVELING WORKS

Assess. Access. Lift. Verify.

The homeowner-level process is straightforward even though the technical details vary by method and slab condition.

01

Measure settlement, drainage, cracking and fixed elevations.

02

03

Lift

04

Verify

Recheck elevation, transitions, drainage and repaired access points.

METHODS

Mudjacking or polyurethane?

Both can lift concrete. The research specifically rejects treating either method as universally superior.

MUDJACKING / SLABJACKING

Cementitious slurry below the slab.

Ask about suitability, injection-hole size, visible patches, use time, limitations and expected longevity for your surface.

Explore mudjacking →
POLYURETHANE LIFTING

Expanding material below the slab.

Ask why it fits the slab, how lift is controlled, what void-filling is expected and what the warranty actually covers.

BEFORE YOU HIRE

Make the quote explain the problem.

A good estimate should tell you more than the price.

  1. What do you believe caused the settlement?
  2. Why is this slab suitable for leveling instead of replacement?
  3. Which lifting method would you use here, and why?
  4. What result is realistically achievable at the garage, step or drainage transition?
  5. What is included, excluded and covered if movement returns?
